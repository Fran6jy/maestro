The cost of being right

I rebuilt the trading strategies from my MSc, added two reference points, and tested all eight the same way on three years of EUR/USD prices. Several of them find a real pattern in the market. None of them earns enough to pay for trading it.

The claim

My MSc reported a 37.6% hit rate. It doesn't hold up.

That number came from one month of 5-minute prices, and the model was scored on the same data it learned from. The Sharpe ratio of 0.599 quoted next to it came from a different test altogether, a daily moving-average strategy run from 2014 to 2023.

I re-ran the same regression on the same month. It scores 46.6%. The difference comes from how the score treats 5-minute bars where the price doesn't move: it counts them as wrong. Round the prices to four decimal places and many more bars stop moving, so the score falls to 30.6%. The number says as much about the data feed as about the model.

The test

Same data, same rules, same costs for every strategy

Each month, a strategy learns only from the months before it, then trades the next month without seeing it. I repeated that for every month from January 2023 to March 2026, 39 times in all. Every trade pays 0.8 pips, the typical spread on EUR/USD.

The cost

The edge is real. It's smaller than the cost.

Before costs, most of these strategies make a little money on each trade. The best, Bollinger bands, makes about half a pip. Each trade costs 0.8 pips. The strategies that trade most often, tens of thousands of times, lose the most.

How this was tested

Prices
OANDA EUR/USD mid prices, 5-minute bars, from January 2022 to March 2026.
Walk-forward
39 monthly test windows. Each strategy trains only on data before its test month, with a 5-day gap in between.
Costs
0.8 pips per round trip, the typical EUR/USD spread in MAESTRO's cost model. One unit of EUR/USD, no leverage.
Timing
A decision made at the close of one bar acts on the next bar. Automated tests confirm no strategy can see a future price.
Settings
Moving averages 20 and 200 bars. Contrarian over 3 bars. Bollinger bands over 20 bars at 2 standard deviations. Regressions use the last 1 or 5 moves. The MSc report does not state the contrarian and Bollinger settings, so standard defaults are used.
Code
backtesting/baselines.py and tests/test_baselines.py in the MAESTRO repository.
MSc source
My MSc project report, “A Comparative Study of Machine Learning Approaches for Predictive Modeling in Algorithmic Trading”, Coventry University, 2022/23.
